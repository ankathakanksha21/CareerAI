# Step 7: Peers Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** follow `careerai-building-a-step` stage 4. Every task is test-first: write the tests, watch them fail for the right reason, write the code, watch them pass, run the whole suite on its own, commit. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Status, 15 October 2026:** plan written; waiting for the owner's approval.

**Goal:** A user who switches Peers on is matched by the app into a buddy group of two or three. Members see only each other's first name, university, course, target roles, how many weekly targets they have met and their streak. They can nudge, write one check-in line a week, share a LinkedIn or WhatsApp link, and leave, block or report at any time. Nobody is ever invented: with no match, the user waits.

**Architecture:** One pure module, `peers.py`: the field list, code of conduct, report reasons, nudge wording, contact-link rules, ranking for matching, progress, group streak and inactivity. One SQL module, `peerdb.py`, holds every peers query. **Its `peer_view` is the only function in the codebase that returns information about another user.** Matching also reads other users' rows (field, places, university, time waited, blocks), but it returns only the group it made, and what anyone then sees comes through `peer_view`. `repo.py` keeps its rule that every function touches only the caller's rows, which is why peers SQL lives apart from it. `ai.py` gains one task, `suggest_field`. The routes in `main.py` stay thin. `GET /api/state` carries the caller's peer status, so the Peers page and the Home card refresh with everything else.

**Tech stack:** unchanged. No new packages, outside services or settings.

**Step document:** `.claude/skills/careerai-step-7-peers/SKILL.md`, including the owner's answers of 15 October 2026.

## Global constraints

- Everything in `CLAUDE.md` applies. The points that bite hardest here:
- **The one exception.** Only `peerdb.peer_view` returns another user's information. It builds its result from an explicit list of columns, never `select *`. Every other function returns only the caller's rows, or ids and yes/no answers.
- **What peers see:** first name, university, course, target roles. Members of an active group, once both have accepted, also see targets met this week (`{"met": n, "of": 3}`), the streak in weeks, the contact link, this week's check-in, and whether the member has been inactive for three weeks.
- **Never shown:** surname, email, visa type or dates, applications, employers, CVs, achievements, journal, transcripts, any dates of the member's activity, or their user id. Pages refer to a member by the id of their membership row, never by user id.
- **No fake peers.** No seeded users and no sample groups. With nobody to match, the page says "You are on the list".
- **Tests share the real database with real accounts.** Real users may be waiting in Peers. A test that expects two test users to be paired gives both the same unique city (`Zzqxtown-<uuid>`) and the university `Zzqx University`, so they outrank any real user. A test never asserts that a user stays waiting after matching runs, unless it replaces `peerdb._candidates` with one returning nothing. No test counts or clears a whole table.
- **Every new table and route** gets the standing tests: rows deleted with the account, RLS on, no browser privileges; 401 without a token, 422 for a malformed id, 404 for a group the caller is not in; the daily AI limit on the field suggestion.
- British English, no em dashes in UI copy or prompts.

### Task 4: Matching, answering a proposal, expiry and leaving

**Files:**
- Modify: `backend/peerdb.py`, `backend/tests/test_peerdb.py`

**Interfaces:**
- Produces:
  - `_candidates(conn, user_id, me: dict) -> list[dict]`: people waiting with Peers on, not barred, not the caller, with no block or pass either way; and active groups with exactly two accepted members, no proposed member, every member in the caller's field, no block or pass with any member. Rows are locked with `for update skip locked`. Shaped for `peers.rank`.
  - `propose(conn, user_id) -> str | None`: if the caller is waiting, ranks the candidates and either creates a proposed group of two (slots 1 and 2) or adds the caller to a group with room (the free slot); clears `waiting_since` for everyone proposed. A `UniqueViolation` inside a savepoint means someone else took them first: the caller stays waiting. Returns the group id or None.
  - `switch_on` now calls `propose`.
  - `answer(conn, user_id, group_id, *, yes: bool) -> None`: raises `LookupError` when the caller has no live row in that group or there is nothing for them to answer. With the group row locked: if the caller's row is proposed, yes adds them to `yes_from` of every live row; if they are accepted and a newcomer is proposed, yes adds them to the newcomer's `yes_from`. A proposed row whose `yes_from` holds every live member becomes accepted (`joined_at = now()`); a group with two accepted becomes active (`active_since` = London today). No: the proposed row (the caller's own, or the newcomer they turned down) becomes declined; passes are written (decision 5); a group left with fewer than two live members closes; everyone who is no longer in a live group goes back on the list and `propose` runs for each.
